# Somebody else, and you too

The **Low** face of the feeling picker. An original self-kindness script, about
**7 minutes** read at a slow pace with the pauses honoured.

Written 19 September 2026, and rewritten the same day. Nothing in it is taken
from any published script. The images -- a hand over the heart, kind words said
plainly, warmth sent outward before inward -- are common contemplative and
compassion-focused material with no owner. Every sentence here was written for
this file. See the rules section before changing a line.

Previously titled "Your own hand". The hand is still the anchor and the last
line, but it is no longer the main move, so the title moved with the script.

---

## Why this face is not a breathing screen and not a body scan

Low mood is kept going by **rumination** -- going over why you feel bad, again
and again. Nolen-Hoeksema's response-styles work is the long-running evidence
for it: a ruminative response to low mood predicts longer and worse episodes.

That rules out two obvious shapes:

| Shape | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| A long body scan | Slow, inward, unstructured attention is rumination's favourite shape. |
| "Sit with the sadness and look at it" | Noticing turns into chewing over, and the chewing is the mechanism. |

So attention in this script is either on **weight and warmth** (physical, no
story attached), on **somebody else**, or **wide** (the air, the feet, the
room). The script never asks why, and there is no line anywhere about what
caused this.

## Why self-kindness

Self-kindness is the right target. Low mood arrives loud with self-blame, and
low self-compassion tracks depression strongly across studies (MacBeth &
Gumley, *Clinical Psychology Review*, 2012). Kindness practices raise positive
feeling, which is the part of mood that goes flat (Fredrickson et al., 2008).

Neff's account of self-compassion has three parts: self-kindness, **common
humanity** -- the sense that this is a human thing rather than a fault in you --
and mindfulness. The first draft of this script had the first and the third and
nothing at all of the second, and that was the hole this rewrite fills.

Common humanity matters most for exactly this reader. Low mood says *it is only
me, and I am broken*. Warm words alone do not answer that. Another person in the
script does.

## Why the kindness goes outward first

The reader wishes an easy day on somebody they care about, and only then are
they included in the same words.

| Reason | Evidence or mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Aiming kindness at yourself stings | Gilbert's fear-of-compassion work finds that fear highest in people who are low and self-critical. Neff calls the sting backdraft. |
| Wishing well on somebody else is easy | It is the standard clinical route in. Neff & Germer's MSC and Gilbert's CFT both start warm attention on another and turn it inward afterwards. |
| It supplies common humanity | The other person is having their own day. That is the bridge, and it is the ingredient the first draft lacked. |

**The first draft rejected loving-kindness outright, and that rejection no
longer holds.** It was rejected for three reasons. The outward-first order fixes
one of them, and the other two are fixed by wording rather than by dropping the
shape:

| Original objection | What is done about it |
| --- | --- |
| Aiming kindness at yourself stings | Solved by the order. The reader is warm before they are included. |
| "Bring someone you love to mind" is a recall task | Cut the search. "Whoever turns up first" removes the rummaging and the ranking. |
| Warmth becomes the reader's job | The wishes carry no "for you" and are never checked. The reader says words, and never has to produce a feeling. |

**What is still rejected is the loving-kindness machinery**: the set phrases
("may I be happy, may I be safe"), the rings of people ending in a difficult
person, and the requirement to feel anything. The evidence for classic
loving-kindness is also thinner than its reputation -- meta-analyses (Galante
2014; Zeng 2015) find good effects on positive feeling and self-compassion, and
only small-to-moderate effects on depression, from small studies.

## The kindness arrives, it is not generated

This is still the decision the script turns on.

| Loving-kindness | Here |
| --- | --- |
| The reader generates the warmth | The reader says plain words, and their own palm supplies the warmth |
| The kind phrases are aimed at the self first | They are aimed outward first, then widened to include the self |
| Failure is possible | There is nothing to produce and nothing is checked |

**The character carries the company, and no line names her.** She is on screen
sitting with the reader, doing nothing. The script never mentions her, because
a line about a character on screen cannot be heard with closed eyes, and a line
asking the reader to imagine somebody beside them is a task -- and a cruel one
if their answer is nobody.

## Read or listen -- one script, two ways

Same four rules as `wound-up-tighten-and-stop.md`:

- **No line may require closed eyes.** "Your eyes can close, or stay open and
  go loose" is the only line that touches them, and both answers are equal.
  Nothing at the end asks the reader to open them.
- **No line may name the screen, the voice, or the sound.** That is also why
  the widening section uses air, feet and space rather than sounds in the room.
- **Pauses carry both modes.** Heard, a pause is silence. Read, it is how long
  the line stays before the next one arrives.
- **Every line fits one glance.** The longest is eleven words.

## The shape

| Part | Job | Lines | Approx |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Settling | Arrive. Give the permission. | 7 | 0:56 |
| 2. Held | The chair takes the weight. | 4 | 0:44 |
| 3. Somebody else | The kind words go outward, where they are easy. | 7 | 1:15 |
| 4. Your own hand | Warm touch. The hinge between outward and inward. | 5 | 0:53 |
| 5. And you too | The same words, now including the reader. | 8 | 1:40 |
| 6. Wider | Attention goes out, off the chest and off the story. | 4 | 0:50 |
| 7. Leaving | Soft, and with no job. | 5 | 0:52 |

Forty lines, about 7:10. `[pause Ns]` is a held silence, not a read line.

**Seven minutes, not ten.** The mountain script can run ten and a half because
it is opened by somebody calm who chose to sit down. Low mood comes with poor
task persistence, so length here is a real risk of abandonment. The first draft
ran 6:25; the outward section is what added the extra forty seconds, and nothing
else was allowed to grow.

---

## 1. Settling

Find a way to sit that you could hold for a while.

Let your back be tall, and let your shoulders drop.

Rest your hands wherever they land.

[pause 5s]

You can stop whenever you want. Nothing here has to be finished.

[pause 5s]

Your eyes can close, or stay open and go loose.

[pause 8s]

It is a low day.

You do not have to explain it to anyone.

[pause 10s]

## 2. Held

Feel the chair, or the floor, holding your weight.

It is doing all the work.

[pause 8s]

Let yourself be heavy on it.

[pause 8s]

Nothing has to be lifted right now.

[pause 12s]

## 3. Somebody else

Now let somebody you care about come to mind.

Whoever turns up first. A person, or an animal.

[pause 8s]

They are somewhere else right now, having their own day.

[pause 8s]

Some plain words for them. There is nothing to answer.

[pause 5s]

I hope today is easy.

[pause 8s]

I hope you are alright.

[pause 8s]

You are allowed to feel exactly what you feel.

[pause 10s]

## 4. Your own hand

Now bring one hand up, and rest it on the middle of your chest.

[pause 5s]

Let it sit there with some weight.

[pause 8s]

Feel the warmth of your palm coming through.

[pause 10s]

This is your own hand.

It has been warm all day.

[pause 10s]

## 5. And you too

Now somebody who has had this feeling all day.

[pause 5s]

That is you.

[pause 8s]

The same words again, and they are for you as well.

[pause 5s]

I hope today is easy.

[pause 10s]

I hope you are alright.

[pause 10s]

You are allowed to feel exactly this much.

[pause 10s]

Right now, somebody else is feeling exactly this.

[pause 10s]

It is a human way to feel.

[pause 10s]

## 6. Wider

Now let your hand come down, and rest it wherever it lands.

[pause 8s]

Feel the air on your face.

[pause 8s]

Feel your feet on the floor.

[pause 8s]

Feel how much room there is around you.

[pause 10s]

## 7. Leaving

Your feet are on the floor, and your hands are resting.

[pause 8s]

This is how it is today.

[pause 8s]

Stay as long as you want.

[pause 8s]

Nothing is finished, and nothing has to be.

[pause 8s]

Your hand can go back there whenever you want.

## The rules, with the versions that were rejected

Each rule below cost something to find. The wrong version is kept so the rule
does not get quietly undone later.

### The person comes to mind, and is never searched for

"Now let somebody you care about come to mind. / Whoever turns up first."

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Bring to mind someone you love. | A search, and a ranking. Choosing from memory is time travel, which every script in this app bans. |
| Someone who has been good to you. Someone who makes things easier. | The elaboration is what turns an arrival into a task. This is the same fault the mountain script had, and the same fix. |

This is the one place the script goes near memory at all, and it does so by
explicit decision. "Whoever turns up first" is what keeps it an arrival rather
than a hunt. **A named person is worth this cost**: a real person the reader
cares about produces warmth that a stranger does not, and the warmth is the
whole engine of the turn in part 5.

Rejected alternative: *"Somebody, somewhere, is having a day like this one."* No
recall at all, and safe -- but the warmth is thin, so the turn to self in part 5
has nothing to run on.

### The person is alive, and elsewhere

"They are somewhere else right now, having their own day."

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| *(nothing between the arrival and the kind words)* | Without this line, somebody who has died can turn up, and the script then sends kind words after them. |

The line is present tense and it names no death. It does the safety work
quietly, and it doubles as the common-humanity bridge -- they have their own
day, and so does the reader.

### The reader is included, never promoted

"Now somebody who has had this feeling all day. / That is you. / The same words
again, and they are for you as well."

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Now turn the kindness towards yourself. | Makes the self a new, harder section. A low reader then compares: they deserve it, I do not. That sharpens the self-blame this script exists to loosen. |
| And now, the hardest one. Yourself. | Predicts distress, and scores the attempt before it starts. |

Part 5 opens with the **same grammar** as part 3 -- "Now somebody who..." against
"Now let somebody...". The parallel is what makes the arrival of the reader feel
like inclusion rather than a promotion to a harder exercise.

### The version of you is today's, not childhood's

"Somebody who has had this feeling all day. / That is you."

The reader is offered a small step back from themselves before the kind words
land. That step is the mechanism: a self-critical person can refuse kindness
aimed at "me" and accept it aimed at "somebody who has had a day like that".

**The inner-child version was considered and rejected.** Picturing yourself as a
small child is the classic compassion-focused bridge, and it is used in CFT and
in schema therapy. Three things rule it out here:

| Problem | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Childhood is where the harm often is | For a real share of low readers, the child version of themselves is the scene of abuse, neglect or bullying. Inner-child work is done alongside a therapist, never by an app at two in the morning. |
| It is deep recall | Part 3's recall is light and present -- who do you care about, now. Childhood is the far past, and this app's scripts ban time travel. |
| It is rumination fuel | The whole face is built around Nolen-Hoeksema: no going back over your own story. A child self is an invitation to do exactly that. |

Today's self has none of that, and it has its own evidence. Kross and Ayduk's
self-distancing work finds that a small step back from yourself **reduces**
rumination, where staying immersed in it does not -- which is the same target
this face was designed around.

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Picture yourself as a small child. | Opens childhood, demands a picture, and sends the reader into the past. |
| Think of yourself a few years ago. | Vaguer, still the past, and it invites a comparison with how things were. |
| Imagine a younger you sitting there. | "Imagine" is banned app-wide, and it fails for anybody who pictures nothing. |

**Nothing is visualised.** The line describes a person by what happened to them
today, so a reader with no mind's eye follows it exactly as well as anybody
else.

### Shared humanity is one person, right now

"Right now, somebody else is feeling exactly this. / It is a human way to feel."

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Other people are feeling this today. | A crowd, and a crowd is abstract. Vague things are a puzzle, and solving a puzzle is leaving the room. |
| You are not the only one. | To hear it, the reader must first build "I am the only one". The pink elephant problem, in the one line meant to cure it. |
| Everybody feels like this sometimes. | Overclaims, and reads as brushing it off. |
| The person you thought of has felt it too. | A claim about a real person that the script cannot know, and it can be plainly wrong. |

One person, present tense, and "exactly this" rather than "something like it".
That is the line that answers *nobody knows what this is like*. The second line
frames it afterwards. Specific first, general second -- never the other way
round.

### The wishes carry no "for you"

"I hope today is easy." / "I hope you are alright."

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| May you be well. May you be at ease. | Temple language. It makes a reader self-conscious and it is loving-kindness machinery. |
| I hope today is easy **for you**. | Pins the words to one person, so the same line cannot be reused in part 5. The echo between parts 3 and 5 is the mechanism; a pinned line breaks it. |

The lines are word-for-word the same in both parts, on purpose. Repetition
settles. A synonym would be writing for a reader with their eyes open.

### The permission is standing, and it is early

Line four, in the settling: "You can stop whenever you want. Nothing here has
to be finished."

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| *(before part 4)* If putting a hand on your chest feels like too much, skip it. | It is a decision, mid-session, and it predicts distress right before the thing it warns about. A permission needs no answer and is still true five minutes later. |

Backdraft is real -- some people well up the moment kindness is aimed at them.
The standing permission covers it. A warning would plant it.

### The warmth is physical, never emotional

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Let a feeling of warmth spread through your chest. | That is a feeling to produce. A palm is already warm, so nothing can be missing. |
| Notice how it feels to be kind to yourself. | Comments on how the reader is doing, and scores the session. |

### Heavy is used once, not six times

The first draft said "heavy" six times, across three parts. Heaviness is what
low mood already feels like, so leaning on it hands the reader their own symptom
as the main image. One use is enough to make it allowed. The other five became
"drop", "with some weight", "holding your weight" and "resting".

### An animal counts

"Whoever turns up first. A person, or an animal."

Isolation is a core feature of low mood, and a script that fails at line one for
somebody with nobody is a bad script. The pet is standard in compassion
training for exactly this reason. It is offered as words, like a body-sensation
menu, rather than as a question to answer.

### Nothing checks, and nothing scores

There is no line anywhere asking whether it worked, whether they feel warmer,
or whether they feel better than when they sat down.

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Notice if anything has softened. | A test. Somebody who feels worse has failed it. |
| Notice how much easier that was for them than for you. | Names the gap, and turns the whole script into evidence against the reader. |
| You showed up for yourself today. | A mark out of ten, dressed as kindness. |

### No claim about tomorrow

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| This will not last forever. | A promise the script cannot keep, to somebody with no reason to believe it. |
| Low days pass, the way weather does. | Same promise with scenery on it. |

### No line plants what it means to rule out

| Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- |
| You do not have to earn kind words. | To hear it, the reader builds "I have to earn kindness". This was in the first draft and it broke the file's own rule. Cut, not replaced -- part 5 already says enough. |
| It is not your fault. | Same fault. The reader has to build the fault first. |
| Nothing out there needs anything from you this minute. | A promise about the world that the script cannot keep, and it plants a world full of demands. Cut from part 6. |

### Two lines were cut to pay for part 5

Part 5 grew by four lines when the self-distancing opening and the second
shared-humanity line went in. The length was held near seven minutes by cutting,
not by shortening pauses.

| Cut | Why it was the right thing to lose |
| --- | --- |
| *(part 2)* Notice that you are breathing. / You do not have to change it. | Part 2's job is "the chair takes the weight". The breath lines were a second job, and a part that cannot state its job in one sentence is doing two things. |
| *(part 5)* You do not have to be any different right now. | It says the same thing as "You are allowed to feel exactly this much", which is the stronger of the two. |

### No line asks why

Searched and confirmed: the words "why", "because", "cause" and "reason" do not
appear in the script. Rumination is the mechanism this face is designed around.

### Attention does not park on the chest

Only **part 4** puts attention on the chest itself, and it runs 0:53. The hand
stays there through part 5, but attention there is on a person and on words,
never on the sensation -- part 5's first line takes the reader off their body
entirely. Part 6 then goes out to the air, the feet and the space, and the hand
comes down at its first line. Anxious readers can open any tab, and held
attention on the chest feeds the fear loop.

### App-wide bans, checked

- No "deep breath", "breathe deeply", "fill your lungs", "big breath in".
- No "let go", "release".
- No "see", "picture" or "imagine". Part 3 asks who, never what they look like.
  Part 5 describes a person by what happened to them today. Somebody who
  pictures nothing follows every line exactly as well as anybody else.
- Every "not", "no" and "nothing" removes a task rather than planting an image.

### The last line has no job in it

"Your hand can go back there whenever you want." It offers the one thing the
reader now owns, and then stops. Rejected: "Use your hand whenever you need
it today" -- that is homework.

## One small thing -- the screen after, not the last line

Behavioural activation is the strongest single lever for low mood: doing one
small thing matches full cognitive therapy for depression in the COBRA trial
(Richards et al., *Lancet*, 2016). It does not belong inside the script,
because the closing line may not hand the reader a task.

So it is a **separate soft screen after the script ends**, and it is optional:

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| One suggestion, not a list | A list is a menu, and choosing is work. |
| Tiny and physical -- stand up, open a curtain, fill a glass of water | Big suggestions get refused, and a refusal is a failure. |
| "Not now" carries the same weight as the suggestion | Both answers have to be real answers. |
| Nothing is stored, counted or compared | A count turns a low week into a failed test. |

Not built yet. It is the second half of this face and it is worth building.

`Feeling.low` currently leads nowhere: `_pick` in

`lib/app/core/app_constants.dart`, a view, and one line in `_pick`.

The shape animates through the pauses, so a reader is never left looking at a
