## **17 UNHELPFUL THINKING STYLES**

---

### **1\. MIND READING**

You assume that you know what people think without having sufficient evidence of their thoughts.  
*Example:* “He thinks I’m a loser.”

---

### **2\. FORTUNE TELLING**

You predict the future—that things will get worse or that there is danger ahead.  
*Example:* “I’ll fail that exam” or “I won’t get the job.”

---

### **3\. CATASTROPHISING**

You believe that what has happened or will happen will be so awful and unbearable that you won’t be able to stand it.  
*Example:* “It would be terrible if I failed.”

---

### **4\. LABELLING**

You assign global negative traits to yourself and others.  
*Example:* “I’m undesirable” or “He’s a rotten person.”

---

### **5\. DISCOUNTING POSITIVES**

You claim that the positive accomplishments you or others attain are trivial.  
*Example:* “That’s what wives are supposed to do—so it doesn’t count” or “Those successes were easy, so they don’t matter.”

---

### **6\. NEGATIVE FILTER**

You focus almost exclusively on the negatives and seldom notice the positives.  
*Example:* “Look at all of the people who don’t like me.”

---

### **7\. OVERGENERALISING**

You perceive a global pattern of negatives on the basis of a single incident.  
*Example:* “This generally happens to me. I seem to fail at a lot of things.”

---

### **8\. DICHOTOMOUS THINKING**

You view events, or people, in all-or-nothing terms.  
*Example:* “I get rejected by everyone” or “It was a waste of time.”

---

### **9\. “SHOULDS”**

You interpret events in terms of how things *should* be rather than focusing on what is.  
*Example:* “I should do well. If I don’t, then I’m a failure.”

---

### **10\. “WHAT IFS”**

You ask a series of questions about “what if” something happens and are never satisfied with the answers.  
*Example:* “Yeah, but what if I get anxious?” or “What if I can’t catch my breath?”

---

### **11\. PERSONALISING**

You attribute a disproportionate amount of blame for negative events to yourself and fail to see that others may be involved.  
*Example:* “My marriage ended because I failed.”

---

### **12\. BLAMING**

You focus on the other person as the source of your negative feelings and refuse to take responsibility for your own change.  
*Example:* “She’s to blame for the way I feel now” or “My parents caused all my problems.”

---

### **13\. UNFAIR COMPARISONS**

You interpret events using unrealistic standards by focusing on people who do better than you, and then judge yourself harshly.  
*Example:* “She’s more successful than I am” or “Others did better than I did on the test.”

---

### **14\. REGRET ORIENTATION**

You dwell on how you could have done better in the past rather than what you can do now.  
*Example:* “I could have had a better job if I had tried” or “I shouldn’t have said that.”

---

### **15\. EMOTIONAL REASONING**

You let your feelings dictate your perception of reality.  
*Example:* “I feel depressed; therefore, my marriage isn’t working out.”

---

### **16\. INABILITY TO DISCONFIRM**

You reject any evidence that contradicts your negative thoughts.  
*Example:* “I’m unlovable,” while ignoring all signs that people care about you.  
Alternate Example: “That’s not the real issue. There are deeper problems.”

---

### **17\. JUDGEMENT FOCUS**

You see yourself, others, and events in black-and-white, good-bad, or superior-inferior terms. You’re constantly measuring yourself and others and often feel like you or they fall short.  
*Example:* “I didn’t perform well in college” or “Look how successful she is. I’m not successful.”
