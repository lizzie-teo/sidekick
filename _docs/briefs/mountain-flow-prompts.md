# Google Flow prompts — the mountain screen

For visualising each element of `mountain-visual.md` before rebuilding it in
Rive. **Nothing generated here ships.** These are sketches to look at and argue
with.

Written 19 September 2026.

---

## How to use this page

1. Paste the **style block** at the top of every prompt.
2. Paste **one element prompt** under it.
3. Paste the **negatives** at the bottom.
4. Generate. Expect to re-roll two or three times.

Veo will fight you on two things every single time:

- **It wants to move the camera.** It adds slow pushes and drifts by default.
- **It wants drama.** Sun rays, lightning, a swell when the storm clears.

Both are banned on this screen. If a take has either, throw it out rather than
talking yourself into it.

---

## The style block

Paste this first, every time.

```
Flat 2D vector animation. Minimal. Mobile portrait, 9:16.
Limited flat colour palette, three colours maximum, no more.
Solid flat shapes with clean edges. No texture, no noise, no grain.
Not photorealistic. Not 3D. Not painterly.
The look of a screen-printed poster, or a paper cut-out.
Locked static camera. The camera never moves.
Extremely slow, calm, steady motion throughout.
Silent. No audio, no music, no sound effects.
```

---

## The negatives

Paste this last, every time.

```
No sun, no sun disc, no sun rays, no lens flare, no god rays.
No moon, no stars. No birds, no animals, no people, no butterflies.
No trees, no forest, no grass, no flowers, no snow caps, no rock detail.
No horizon line, no foreground, no ground, no path, no water.
No text, no logos, no watermarks, no letterboxing.
No lightning, no rain streaks, no falling rain, no snow falling.
No camera shake, no zoom, no pan, no tilt, no dolly, no parallax.
No fast motion, no time lapse, no dramatic lighting, no colour grading shift.
```

# Stills, for drawing from

**Start here if you are drawing the shapes.** The clips further down are for

Use the image side of Flow, not the video side.

## The still style block

Replaces the video one above. The motion and audio lines are gone.

```
Flat 2D vector illustration. Minimal. Mobile portrait, 9:16.
Limited flat colour palette, three colours maximum, no more.
Solid flat shapes with clean edges. No texture, no noise, no grain.
Not photorealistic. Not 3D. Not painterly. No shading, no shadows.
The look of a screen-printed poster, or a paper cut-out.
```

Keep the **same negatives** as the video prompts. They all still apply.

## S1. The cloud sheet

The most useful single prompt on this page. It gives you shapes to trace.

```
A set of six separate cloud shapes, arranged in two rows of three,
on a plain flat background. Each cloud is a different shape.
Every cloud is one solid flat colour with a soft rounded edge
and no outline, no gradient and no detail inside it.
Each cloud is clearly wider than it is tall.
None of the clouds is symmetrical — every one is heavier at one end.
There is no sky, no mountain and nothing else in the image.
```

**What to look for:** run this four or five times and take the best two or
three clouds from each sheet. You want five keepers in the end, and they
should not look like a family.

**The symmetry line is the one that matters.** Generators default to
symmetrical puffs, and a symmetrical cloud reads as a shape somebody made.
If a sheet comes back symmetrical, re-roll it.

---

## S2. The mountain shape

```
A single dark mountain silhouette fills the lower half of the frame.
One peak. Wide base. Centred. Set against a pale, plain, empty sky.
The mountain is one solid flat shape with nothing drawn inside it —
no ridge lines, no shading, no snow, no rocks, no outline.
Nothing else is in the image.
```

Run it several times with the variants further down this page, and put the
results side by side.

---

## S3. The whole scene, light

What the screen looks like for most of the session.

```
A dark mountain silhouette, one peak, sits in the lower half of the frame
against a pale sky. A few soft pale clouds are spread across the sky
at different heights and sizes, some behind the peak and one across it.
The clouds are irregularly spaced, with real gaps between them.
Everything is flat, with no shading and no outlines.
```

**What to look for:** the spacing. Evenly spread clouds read as a pattern.
This is the picture that tells you whether the composition breathes.

---

## S4. The whole scene, dark

Same shot, other end of the palette.

```
A mountain silhouette sits in the lower half of the frame against the sky.
The sky is a deep, dark, muted colour.
The mountain is darker still, and clearly heavier than the sky.
A few soft clouds, lighter than both, are spread across the sky
at different heights and sizes, irregularly spaced.
Everything is flat, with no shading and no outlines.
```

**The mountain is never lighter than the sky, in any palette.** That one rule
keeps the silhouette reading as a silhouette across all twelve looks.

---

## S5. The storm

```
A dark mountain silhouette sits in the lower half of the frame
against a deep charcoal sky.
One or two large, flat, heavy cloud masses spread across most of the sky,
wide and low, with soft rounded edges and no detail inside them.
There is no rain, no lightning and no bright area anywhere in the image.
The picture is dark and even, and not dramatic.
```

**What to look for:** put it next to S3. **Only the colour should have
changed.** If the storm picture feels busier or more detailed, it has been
performed, and the voice brief bans that.

# Clips, for judging motion

Everything below makes 8-second video. Use it after the shapes are settled, to
check speeds — especially that the storm moves at the **same** speed as the
calm weather.

## 1. The mountain, still

The anchor shot. Everything else is judged against this.

```
A single dark mountain silhouette fills the lower half of the frame.
One peak. Wide base. Centred. Set against a pale, plain, empty sky.
The mountain is one solid flat shape with nothing drawn inside it.
Nothing moves at all. The shot is completely still for the whole clip.
```

**What to look for:** is the silhouette a *shape* or a *place*? If you can see
where it is, it is wrong. Detail creeping in — a ridge line, a shadow on one
face, a hint of snow — is the failure to watch for.

---

## 2. The empty sky

Part A. Before the mountain exists.

```
A pale empty sky fills the whole frame.
Three soft horizontal bands of very pale cloud drift slowly
from the left edge to the right edge, each at a slightly different speed.
There is no mountain, no ground and no horizon. Only sky.
The drift is so slow it is barely perceptible.
```

**What to look for:** does it feel alive without asking to be watched? If your
eye keeps going to a particular cloud, the contrast is too high.

---

## 3. The mountain with weather

Parts B to E. This is the screen for most of the ten minutes.

```
A dark mountain silhouette, one peak, sits perfectly still
in the lower half of the frame against a pale sky.
Soft pale cloud bands drift slowly from left to right,
passing behind and across the peak at slightly different speeds.
The mountain never moves. Not at all. Only the sky moves.
```

**What to look for:** the mountain must be visibly, obviously the still thing.
If it breathes, drifts or wobbles even slightly, that is the one rule broken.
The whole script rests on it.

---

## 4. The one cloud

Takes **D5**, **D6** and **D7**: "watch one arrive / watch it move across /
watch it thin out, and go."

```
A single soft pale cloud enters from the left edge of the frame.
It crosses slowly behind a still dark mountain peak.
Near the right edge it thins out and disappears.
The whole crossing takes the entire clip, at one steady speed.
The mountain does not move.
The cloud is one soft flat shape with a slightly fuzzy edge.
```

**What to look for:** it must arrive, cross and go in three readable stages,
because the voice names all three. If it is gone before "watch it move across"
would land, it is too fast.

---

## 5. The push in

Part C, take **C1**: "let the mountain come closer, until you are sitting
inside it."

**Delete the "locked static camera" line from the style block for this one.**
It is the only shot with any camera move in the whole screen.

```
A dark mountain silhouette against a pale sky.
The camera pushes in extremely slowly and steadily,
until the base of the mountain passes below the bottom edge of the frame
and the peak fills the upper half.
The move is perfectly even and takes the entire clip.
It does not speed up, slow down, or stop.
```

**What to look for:** even speed. Any ease-in or ease-out makes it a cinematic
move, and a cinematic move is a thing to watch.

---

## 6. The storm

Part F, takes **F1** to **F4**. The hardest one to get right.

```
A dark mountain silhouette sits perfectly still against the sky.
Over the full clip the sky slowly darkens, from pale grey to deep charcoal.
A thick band of flat dark cloud thickens and spreads
until it covers most of the sky, drifting slowly left to right.
There is no rain, no lightning and no camera movement.
The change is gradual and undramatic from start to finish.
The mountain does not move or change at any point.
```

**What to look for:** put this next to prompt 3 and play them one after the
other. **The speed and the amplitude of the motion must be identical.** Only
the colour should differ. If the storm feels faster or busier, Veo has
performed it, and the voice brief bans that.

---

## 7. The clearing

Part F, takes **F5** to **F9**.

```
A dark mountain silhouette sits perfectly still against a deep charcoal sky.
Over the full clip the sky slowly lightens back to pale grey.
The thick cloud band thins and drifts apart.
There is no sunburst, no rays and no bright moment anywhere in the clip.
The sky simply returns to how it was,
at exactly the same slow rate it darkened.
The mountain does not move or change at any point.
```

**What to look for:** Veo will want to give you a sunrise. It is the single
most likely thing to go wrong on this page. If there is a warm moment, a glow,
or anything that feels like relief, throw the take out.

---

## 8. The fade

Part G, take **G5**: "let the mountain fade now."

```
A dark mountain silhouette against a pale sky fades out to nothing.
When it has gone, only the empty sky remains,
with faint pale cloud bands still drifting slowly.
The fade takes the whole clip and is perfectly even throughout.
The mountain does not move as it fades.
```

**What to look for:** it should fade, not dissolve, shrink or fly away.

# Variants to ask for

These answer the open questions in `mountain-visual.md`. Run prompt **1** three
or four times with one line swapped each time, and put the results side by side.

| Question | Swap this line in |
| --- | --- |
| One peak, or a ridge? | `One peak.` → `A long ridge with three uneven peaks.` |
| Flat sky, or a gradient? | `a pale, plain, empty sky` → `a pale sky with a very shallow vertical gradient, lighter at the top` |
| How high does the peak sit? | `fills the lower half of the frame` → `fills the lower two thirds of the frame` |
| Sharp peak, or soft? | add `The peak is sharp and angular.` or `The peak is soft and rounded.` |

**One peak is what the script needs.** Take C2 says "your head is the peak",
and a ridge gives the listener three heads. Generate the ridge anyway so you
can see what you are giving up.

# Dark mode

The app has six palettes in light and dark. Generate the light version first,
get the shape right, then re-run the chosen prompt with this swapped in:

```
The sky is a deep, dark, muted colour.
The mountain is darker still, and clearly heavier than the sky.
```

**The mountain is never lighter than the sky, in any palette.** That one rule
keeps the silhouette reading as a silhouette everywhere.

---

# Judging what comes back

You are picking a **shape and a speed**, not a picture. In that order:

1. **Is the mountain genuinely still?** If not, nothing else matters.
2. **Is it a shape, or a place?** Detail means it is a place.
3. **Does the storm move at the same speed as the calm weather?** Play them back
   to back.
4. **Is there any moment that rewards looking up?** There must not be. Most
   listeners have their eyes closed.
5. Only then: does it look good.

`_docs/skills/lively-motion.md` and `_docs/skills/breathing-animation.md` are
the craft references once you are in Rive. This page only gets you to the
point of knowing what to build.

---

# Taking it into Rive

What survives the trip, and what does not:

| From Flow | Into Rive |
| --- | --- |
| The mountain silhouette | One path. Trace it by hand. Do not import. |
| The sky | A flat fill or a two-stop gradient, bound to the palette. |
| Cloud bands | Three or four soft shapes on **mismatched, non-round periods**, so the sky never visibly loops in ten minutes. See `breathing-animation.md`. |
| The one cloud | Its own timeline, started by the viewmodel at D5. |
| The storm | Colour keys on the sky and the cloud band. No new shapes. |
| The push in | Artboard scale, linear, no easing. |

**Trace, do not trace over.** A Flow frame is a reference for proportion and
mood. Importing it brings the texture and detail the brief spent a page
removing.

**Colour must be bound, not baked.** Six palettes, light and dark, is twelve
looks. Every colour in the file needs to come from the view model.

**Run `/breath-events` if you touch `character.riv`.** This screen should be
its own file, so it should not — but the rule is cheap and the failure is
silent.
