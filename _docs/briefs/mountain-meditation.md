# Mountain meditation

An original script for the Meditate tab. Roughly **10 and a half minutes** read at
a slow pace with the pauses honoured.

It is not Jon Kabat-Zinn's Mountain Meditation and shares no wording with it.
The underlying idea -- sit as a mountain, let the weather pass -- is a public
contemplative image with no owner. His text is copyright and is not used here.
See the rules section at the bottom before changing a line.

## The shape

| Part | Job | Lines | Approx |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Settling | Arrive. Stop doing. | 9 | 1:20 |
| 2. Building the mountain | Make the image. | 6 | 0:55 |
| 3. Becoming the mountain | Put the image in the body. | 8 | 1:10 |
| 4. Clouds | Weather arrives and leaves. The mountain does not move. | 10 | 2:00 |
| 5. Your own weather | Turn it inward. Feelings are the clouds. | 10 | 1:30 |
| 6. Storm | The hard version, and the clearing after it. | 11 | 1:55 |
| 7. Leaving | A clear morning, then out. | 11 | 1:40 |

Sixty-five lines. `[pause Ns]` is a held silence, not a read line.

---

## 1. Settling

Find a way to sit that you could hold for a while.

Let your back be tall, and let your shoulders be heavy.

Rest your hands wherever they land.

Close your eyes, or let your gaze drop and go loose.

[pause 5s]

You can stop whenever you want. Nothing here has to be finished.

[pause 5s]

Feel the chair, or the floor, holding your weight.

It is doing all the work. You do not have to hold yourself up.

[pause 8s]

Notice that you are breathing.

You do not have to change it.

[pause 10s]

## 2. Building the mountain

Now let a mountain come to mind.

[pause 5s]

The peak, high up, with the sky behind it.

The sides, spreading out and down.

The wide base, where the mountain meets the ground.

[pause 8s]

It has been here a long time.

Seasons have come and gone over it.

[pause 10s]

## 3. Becoming the mountain

Now let the mountain come closer, until you are sitting inside it.

Your head is the peak. Quiet, high up, with nothing above it but sky.

Your shoulders and arms are the sides, falling away.

Your legs and the base of you are bedrock, set deep in the ground.

[pause 8s]

Feel the weight of yourself going down into the floor.

You are not holding a pose. You are just sitting, the way a mountain sits.

[pause 10s]

Breathe, and let the mountain breathe with you.

A mountain that breathes, and stays.

[pause 12s]

## 4. Clouds

Now watch the sky around the peak.

Clouds are coming in.

[pause 5s]

Some are thin and pass in seconds. Some are grey and sit for a while.

Some wrap the whole mountain, so you cannot see the top at all.

[pause 8s]

Watch one arrive.

[pause 8s]

Watch it move across.

[pause 8s]

Watch it thin out, and go.

[pause 10s]

The mountain did not push it away. The mountain did not hold on to it.

The cloud came because that is what clouds do. It left for the same reason.

[pause 12s]

## 5. Your own weather

Your feelings are the clouds.

[pause 5s]

See what is in your sky right now.

Maybe something tight. Maybe something sad. Maybe something you cannot name.

Maybe nothing much at all. That is weather too.

[pause 10s]

You do not have to work out where it came from.

You do not have to make it leave.

Let it be there. The mountain does not fix the weather.

[pause 10s]

Watch it move.

Feelings are not solid. They rise, they sit, they thin out.

This one will too. It just needs a bit of time.

[pause 12s]

## 6. Storm

Some days the weather is worse than a cloud.

Rain that does not stop. Wind hard enough to hear. A sky gone black at noon.

[pause 5s]

The mountain takes all of it.

Through the whole thing, it stays where it is.

[pause 8s]

And the storm passes. They always do.

[pause 5s]

The clouds thin out and drift apart.

The mountain comes back into view.

[pause 5s]

Sunlight reaches the peak first, then the sides below it.

The rain is in the ground now, and the trees are growing.

[pause 12s]

The mountain is exactly where it was. Same shape, same stone.

Everything that fell on it soaked in.

[pause 12s]

## 7. Leaving

One more moment with the mountain before you go.

The sun is on it now. The air is clear.

Birds are chirping in the trees again.

Butterflies are chasing each other over the flowers.

[pause 8s]

Let the mountain fade now.

Keep the stillness. That part was yours.

[pause 8s]

Feel the chair again. Feel your hands.

Wiggle your fingers, and your toes.

[pause 5s]

When you are ready, open your eyes.

The weather will keep changing today.

You can let it come and go, the way the mountain does.

## Rules for anyone editing this

**Never tell the reader to take a deep breath.** This is the same rule as the
panic screen, and the reason is the same: a stretched in-breath drops carbon
dioxide and produces breathlessness, dizziness and tingling. Someone anxious
may open this tab. `_docs/affirmation-flow.md` holds the evidence.

**Part 1's "you can stop whenever you want" is the choice point. Do not cut
it as filler.** It is the only line in the script that hands control back.
Sustained attention on the inside can destabilise somebody with trauma or
active dissociation, and what protects against that is the reader keeping
control of what they are doing.

**It sits in part 1, not next to the turn inward, and that placement is the
rule.** It was first written at the top of part 5 -- "if you would rather
stay with the mountain and the sky, stay there" -- on the reasoning that a
way out should arrive just before it is needed. That version was cut, because
it was a **fork**: it stopped somebody four minutes deep and made them decide
between two things. Deciding is work, and work is the one thing this script
is built to remove. It also read as a warning, which plants the distress it
was trying to cover.

Permission is not a fork. "You can stop whenever you want" needs no answer,
costs nothing to hear, and is still true five minutes later. Give it early,
while the reader is still surfaced, and let it stand for the whole session.

**The mountain endures weather. It never models giving in.** Part 6 used to
say "It does not argue with the storm." Read as a mountain, that is just
weather. Read as advice -- and everything in part 6 is read as advice,
because part 5 just taught the reader that the weather is their feelings --
it says do not push back on a bad thing happening to you. Accepting a feeling
is the point of this script. Accepting a situation you could change is
something else, and no meditation gets to tell somebody that. The line is now
"Through the whole thing, it stays where it is": endurance, with nothing said
about resisting.

**Part 7 repeats part 6's imagery on purpose.** The sun, the birds and the
trees come back once, in a clear morning, so the reader carries an open sky
out rather than a storm. Repetition settles; a synonym here would read as a
new scene to build. It stays scenery under the part 6 rule above -- it
describes a morning and never says the reader has one.

**Watch, do not remove.** Part 5 is the one that can go wrong. "Let it pass"
means notice it and stop fighting it. It must never read as "make it go away"
-- that is suppression, which reliably makes feelings stronger and lasts
longer. Every line about a feeling is an instruction to look, never an
instruction to push.

**Do not score the session.** The closing says the weather changes and stops
there. No "well done", no "you are calmer now". Someone who felt worse through
the whole thing has then failed a test they did not sign up for.

**The picture stays cheap, and nothing says "see".** A reader is asked for a
mountain and clouds and nothing else. Every extra detail is another thing to
build, and a mind that cannot build it reads that as failing.

Part 2 used to end on a reassurance -- "if you cannot see it clearly, a sense
of it is enough". It was cut, because a line about how the reader is doing
pulls them out of the thing they are doing. The job it was holding down is
real, though: some people picture nothing at all, and an instruction to *see*
tells them they have already failed. So the job moved into the wording
instead. Part 2 says "let a mountain come to mind", and then names the parts
flatly -- "the peak", "the sides" -- rather than "see the peak". Nothing asks
for a picture, so nothing can be missing.

**Never say the thing you mean to rule out.** Part 3 used to read "Still, but
not dead. Alive, and going nowhere." To understand "not dead" a reader has to
build *dead* first and then knock it down, and the building is the part that
sticks -- the same reason "do not think of a pink elephant" fails. It is one
line now, and it is all positive: "A mountain that breathes, and stays." Say
what is there. Never name what is not.

**Part 6 stays scenery. It must never turn into a lesson.** The sun, the
birds and the trees drinking the rain are there because a storm that only
ends is a flat ending, and because the last thing said before leaving should
be growth rather than damage. What they must never become is a claim about
the reader. One added line -- "and you will grow from this too" -- turns the
whole part into "your suffering is good for you", which is a thing nobody
gets to say to somebody still in it. Part 5 is where this script speaks about
the reader. Part 6 describes a mountain and stops.

**No recall tasks.** The same part used to offer "one you have stood on, one
you have driven past". Choosing between them is a memory search, and a memory
search is a trip out of the present and into the past -- the opposite of what
the next six minutes ask for. Never send the reader looking for something.

**Ten and a half minutes is a normal length, not a problem.** This brief
first said eight minutes was already long, borrowed straight from the panic
script's ten-line rule. That was wrong here. The panic script is short
because working memory is impaired mid-attack; somebody who opened the
Meditate tab on purpose is under no such limit, and ten to fifteen minutes
is the ordinary length of a guided session.

What still holds: no line may be filler, and a part that cannot state its
job in one short sentence is doing two things.
