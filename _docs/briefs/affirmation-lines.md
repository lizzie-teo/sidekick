# Affirmation lines

The lines Home pairs with a pose, and the lines the daily check-in carries in
its own notification text. One set, two readers: somebody who opened the app,
and somebody glancing at a lock screen at 8:30 pm.

Rewritten 19 September 2026, from psychology rather than from philosophy.

## Why this was rewritten

The first two drafts were built from Stoic and then Buddhist material, and
both failed the same way. They were assembled by **subtraction** -- every rule
in the file removed something, so nearly every line removed something too.
"Nothing has to be fixed." "You do not have to believe it." "There is nothing
to finish." Each line is defensible and the set is cold. A reader at 8:30 pm
is handed a list of things that are not required of them, which is not the
same as being handed anything.

A kind affirmation **gives**. Warmth, permission, company, or the plain
acknowledgement that today was hard. That is what this draft is built to do.

## What the psychology actually says

Four findings do all the work here.

### Praise-shaped affirmations hurt the people who need them

Wood, Perunovic and Lee (*Psychological Science*, 2009) had people repeat "I
am a lovable person". People with **low self-esteem felt worse afterwards**,
and worse than a control group who repeated nothing. The statement is so far
from what they believe that saying it starts an argument they lose.

This is the single most important finding for this feature, and it is what
rules out a whole genre:

| Ruled out | Why |
| --- | --- |
| You are enough. | A verdict, and it can be disagreed with. |
| You are doing better than you think. | A claim about the reader that may be plainly false tonight. |
| You are strong. / You've got this. | Praise. Starts the argument Wood found. |
| Believe in yourself. | Homework, and a trait claim. |

Everything above was in the reader's own placeholder set or in common
affirmation apps. None of it survives.

### Kindness works where praise fails

Neff's self-compassion work, and the MacBeth and Gumley meta-analysis
(*Clinical Psychology Review*, 2012), point the other way: **treating
yourself as you would treat somebody you like** tracks better mental health,
where self-esteem boosting does not. Kindness makes no claim about the
reader's worth, so there is nothing to disagree with.

So the lines are kind, not complimentary. "I hope today is easy" cannot be
refuted. "You are amazing" can.

### Validation beats reassurance

Linehan's validation work, and the whole of motivational interviewing, find
that naming somebody's experience accurately lands where reassurance bounces.
"That was a lot to carry" is heard. "It will be fine" is not.

The old draft had almost no validation in it. This one leads with it.

### Affirm a value, not a trait

Self-affirmation theory (Steele, 1988; Cohen and Sherman, *Annual Review*,
2014) has hundreds of studies behind it, and the effect comes from affirming
something the person **cares about** -- not from affirming that they are good
at something. "The things that matter to you still matter" is a value. "You
are capable" is a trait, and a trait can be argued with.

### The app does not know what kind of day it was

Added 19 September 2026, after the third draft. The validation group asserted
a hard day outright -- "That took something out of you. Of course it did." --
and the alert fires **every** evening, on fine days too.

Three things go wrong at once:

| Problem | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| It is often simply untrue | Most days are ordinary. A line insisting today was heavy is wrong more often than right. |
| Inaccurate validation stops working | Validation lands because it is accurate. Linehan's point is that a wrong reading is heard as not being listened to, which is the thing validation exists to fix. |
| It frames a fine day as a bad one | Read over a fortnight, it is an app that thinks the reader is fragile -- and it offers a bad-day reading to somebody who did not have one. |

So no line may assert what today was like. Lines either describe nothing about
the day, say something general and true on any day, or offer a reading
**conditionally** -- "If today was a lot, that makes sense."

**A group of ordinary-day lines was written for this and then cut in the
fourth draft.** See the open questions: the rule above is still held by every
line, but nothing in the set now speaks to a day that went fine.

## The two rules that stayed

Most of the previous draft's bans were my own over-caution and are lifted.
Two are not, and both are safety rules rather than style ones.

1. **No breath instructions.** Not "take a deep breath", not "notice your
   breath". Stretching the in-breath is what hyperventilation looks like, and
   a panic reader may open the app straight after reading one of these lines.
   This is the one ban in the app with a randomised trial behind it.
2. **No promises about tomorrow.** "This will pass", "it gets better". The app
   cannot keep them, and a promise broken by the next bad night costs more
   than the line ever gave.

Everything else -- metaphor, warmth, the word "you", a line that gives an
instruction gently -- is allowed. Lines may run to about twelve words where
warmth needs the room.

**Every line now has an explanation behind it**, written in
`_docs/briefs/affirmation-explanations.md`. That is why the last seven cuts
happened: a line with no rule behind it cannot carry one, and a set where
some lines open something and others do not makes tapping a gamble.

**Validation comes first on purpose.** Somebody who feels believed can hear
the rest. Somebody handed a correction before being believed hears it as
being argued with.

## The warmth group was written, then cut

Two drafts were spent on this and both are worth recording, because the
argument will come round again.

**The ordinary-day lines went first.** "An ordinary day is a perfectly good
day", "Nothing much happened. That is allowed to be nice." They had the same
fault as the hard-day lines they were written to balance, pointing the other
way: they classified today, and the app cannot know what today was.

**The wishes replaced them and then went too.** "I hope tonight is a soft
one", "I am glad you are here", "Hello. I was thinking about you." A wish
claims nothing, so it was safe on any day, and that was the right fix for the
problem in front of it.

It was cut on 19 September 2026 for a different reason. **Tapping an alert
now opens an explanation** -- see `_docs/briefs/affirmation-explanations.md`
-- and a warmth line has nothing to explain. Writing one anyway produces
filler, and filler is what makes an app feel fake. So every line in the set
now has a lesson behind it, and the tap always does the same thing.

**What this costs, stated plainly.** Nothing in the set speaks to a day that
simply went fine, and every line now teaches something. A set that is all
instruction is the failure mode the second draft had, and the warmth group
was the counterweight. If the set ever starts reading as a course rather than
a friend, this decision is the first place to look.

## Why the rights group is the strongest in the file

Lines 4 to 22 come from the legitimate-rights table in
`_docs/briefs/ASSERTIVENESS SKILLS TRAINING l.md`, one line per right. That
tradition runs back to Smith's assertive-rights work in the 1970s, and it is
still the standard handout in assertiveness training.

It clears all three traps this file has hit, which nothing else in the set
manages at once:

| Trap | Why a right clears it |
| --- | --- |
| Praise backfires (Wood 2009) | A right makes no claim about the reader's worth. There is nothing to disagree with, because it is not about how good they are. |
| The app cannot know what kind of day it was | A right is true on a fine Tuesday and a terrible one. It never asserts anything about today. |
| Subtraction reads cold | A right **grants** rather than removes. "You can say no, and leave it at that" hands the reader something. |

There is a fourth reason, and it may be the most useful. **Most evening
distress is about other people** -- something that was said, something agreed
to and now regretted, a message not yet answered. Every other group in this
file speaks to the reader alone. This group is the only one that speaks to the
room they were actually in.

### Written warm, not legal

The source says "You have the right to...". That construction is kept out of
every line.

| Source | Here | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You have the right to say "no." | You can say no, and leave it at that. | "Right" implies somebody grants it, and somebody could refuse. A plain statement needs no permission-giver. |
| You have a right not to have to justify yourself to others. | "I would rather not" is reason enough. | The source names an absence. The line hands over a sentence the reader can actually use. |
| You have a right not to take responsibility for someone else's problem. | Not every problem near you is yours to solve. | Blunt, this reads as permission to abandon people. "Near you" keeps the person in the room. |
| It is selfish to put your needs before others' needs. | You are allowed to come first sometimes. | The source states the mistaken belief in order to argue with it. A line that repeats a mistaken belief plants it. Only the right is used, never the assumption beside it. |

**Ten of the nineteen avoid the word "allowed" entirely**, because nineteen
permissions in a row all built the same way becomes a tic rather than a voice.

## Open questions

- **The rights are nineteen of forty-four.** No longer over half. The thoughts
  group growing from three lines to eleven is what fixed it, rather than
  trimming the rights, which is the better of the two ways.
- **Every line now teaches.** That is the point of the last two rounds of
  cuts, and it is also the risk: a set that only instructs reads as a course
  rather than a friend. Watch for it.
- **Common humanity is one line.** Thin for the idea the Low script leans on
  hardest, and now the smallest group by some way.
- **Lines 19 and 22** -- "Not every problem near you is yours to solve" and
  "Not every message needs an answer tonight" -- are permission to step back.
  For the wrong reader on the wrong night they could read as permission to
  withdraw from everybody, and the warmth that balanced them is gone.
