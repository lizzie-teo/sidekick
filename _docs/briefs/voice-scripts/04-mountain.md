# 04 — Mountain meditation

About **10 minutes 30** on screen. Your recording will be much shorter, because
you do not record the silences.

This is the longest script. The person hearing it chose to sit down and
meditate. They are calm, or at least willing.

## What happens

They picture a mountain. Then they become the mountain. Weather comes and
goes over it. Then their own feelings become the weather.

## Important — every line is its own file

This is **not** one long recording, and we do not cut it up afterwards.

Every line below has a label like **A1** or **F7**. Each label is one take and
one file. Record them one at a time, in order, and stop between them.

What that means for you:

- **The `[PAUSE]` lines are not recorded.** They are not silence you leave in.
  They are the app holding the screen still. You do not have to time anything.
- **A fluffed line costs one line.** Say it again and we keep the better take.
  You never re-run a part.
- **Do not leave silence at the start of a take.** The app plays the file the
  moment the words go on screen, so leading silence looks like a broken app.
- **Leave about half a second at the end** of every take.

There are **64 takes**. That is the most of the four scripts, and they are all
short. It goes faster than it looks.

## Record this one last

The other three set the voice. This is the longest and the most settled, so it
is the one you want to arrive at, not start with.

## Staying the same across 64 takes

This is the real risk on this script, and it is bigger here than anywhere else.
A mountain that does not change is the whole point, so a voice that drifts over
ten minutes undoes it.

- Read the whole script through once out loud before you record anything.
- Record in order, front to back, in **one sitting** if you can.
- Do not move the mic, change the room, or change your distance part way.
- If you must break, break **between parts**, and tell us where.
- Every two parts, play back your first take and match it. Not your last take —
  your first. Matching the last one is how a voice walks away by inches.

## The voice for this script

- Slow, steady, and low. The most settled of the four scripts.
- Even and unhurried the whole way. The mountain does not change, and neither
  does your voice.
- A little more space around each line than the other scripts.

## Do not

- Never say "take a deep breath". It is not in the script. Do not add it.
- Do not do a "meditation voice". No breathiness, no sing-song.
- Do not build drama in the storm part. The storm is described, not performed.
- Do not congratulate at the end. No "well done", no "you did well".

## The storm part — important

Part F has rain, wind and a black sky. It is **scenery**, not drama.

Read it the same way you read the clouds part. Steady. The whole point is that
the mountain does not react, so your voice must not react either.

## This script does ask the person to sit

The low day script does not, on purpose. This one does, and that is deliberate:
a mountain is upright, the listener becomes the mountain, and "your legs and
the base of you are bedrock" does not work lying down.

So the sitting lines stay. Do not soften them.

## How to read the pauses

`[PAUSE 8 SECONDS]` is **not spoken and not recorded**. It tells us how long
the app holds that screen. It is printed so you can see the shape of the
script.

---

# PART A — Settling

**A1**

Find a way to sit that you could hold for a while.

**A2**

Let your back be tall, and let your shoulders be heavy.

**A3**

Rest your hands wherever they land.

**A4**

Close your eyes, or let your gaze drop and go loose.

[PAUSE 5 SECONDS]

**A5**

You can stop whenever you want. Nothing here has to be finished.

[PAUSE 5 SECONDS]

**A6**

Feel the chair, or the floor, holding your weight.

**A7**

It is doing all the work. You do not have to hold yourself up.

[PAUSE 8 SECONDS]

**A8**

Notice that you are breathing.

**A9**

You do not have to change it.

[PAUSE 10 SECONDS]

---

# PART B — Building the mountain

**B1**

Now let a mountain come to mind.

[PAUSE 5 SECONDS]

**B2**

The peak, high up, with the sky behind it.

**B3**

The sides, spreading out and down.

**B4**

The wide base, where the mountain meets the ground.

[PAUSE 8 SECONDS]

**B5**

It has been here a long time.

**B6**

Seasons have come and gone over it.

[PAUSE 10 SECONDS]

Note on B2 to B4: three parts of the mountain, named slowly, one at a time.
They are three separate takes and the app puts a beat between them. Give the
listener time to build each one.

---

# PART C — Becoming the mountain

**C1**

Now let the mountain come closer, until you are sitting inside it.

**C2**

Your head is the peak. Quiet, high up, with nothing above it but sky.

**C3**

Your shoulders and arms are the sides, falling away.

**C4**

Your legs and the base of you are bedrock, set deep in the ground.

[PAUSE 8 SECONDS]

**C5**

Feel the weight of yourself going down into the floor.

**C6**

You are not holding a pose. You are just sitting, the way a mountain sits.

[PAUSE 10 SECONDS]

**C7**

Breathe, and let the mountain breathe with you.

**C8**

A mountain that breathes, and stays.

[PAUSE 12 SECONDS]

---

# PART D — Clouds

**D1**

Now watch the sky around the peak.

**D2**

Clouds are coming in.

[PAUSE 5 SECONDS]

**D3**

Some are thin and pass in seconds. Some are grey and sit for a while.

**D4**

Some wrap the whole mountain, so you cannot see the top at all.

[PAUSE 8 SECONDS]

**D5**

Watch one arrive.

[PAUSE 8 SECONDS]

**D6**

Watch it move across.

[PAUSE 8 SECONDS]

**D7**

Watch it thin out, and go.

[PAUSE 10 SECONDS]

**D8**

The mountain did not push it away. The mountain did not hold on to it.

**D9**

The cloud came because that is what clouds do. It left for the same reason.

[PAUSE 12 SECONDS]

Note on D5, D6 and D7: the same shape said three times. Keep them identical.
Do not vary them for interest. Record all three back to back so they match.

---

# PART E — Your own weather

**E1**

Your feelings are the clouds.

[PAUSE 5 SECONDS]

**E2**

See what is in your sky right now.

**E3**

Maybe something tight. Maybe something sad. Maybe something you cannot name.

**E4**

Maybe nothing much at all. That is weather too.

[PAUSE 10 SECONDS]

**E5**

You do not have to work out where it came from.

**E6**

You do not have to make it leave.

**E7**

Let it be there. The mountain does not fix the weather.

[PAUSE 10 SECONDS]

**E8**

Watch it move.

**E9**

Feelings are not solid. They rise, they sit, they thin out.

**E10**

This one will too. It just needs a bit of time.

[PAUSE 12 SECONDS]

Note on this part: it is the one place the script speaks about the listener. A
touch warmer here than the rest. Only a touch.

---

# PART F — Storm

**F1**

Some days the weather is worse than a cloud.

**F2**

Rain that does not stop. Wind hard enough to hear. A sky gone black at noon.

[PAUSE 5 SECONDS]

**F3**

The mountain takes all of it.

**F4**

Through the whole thing, it stays where it is.

[PAUSE 8 SECONDS]

**F5**

And the storm passes. They always do.

[PAUSE 5 SECONDS]

**F6**

The clouds thin out and drift apart.

**F7**

The mountain comes back into view.

[PAUSE 5 SECONDS]

**F8**

Sunlight reaches the peak first, then the sides below it.

**F9**

The rain is in the ground now, and the trees are growing.

[PAUSE 12 SECONDS]

**F10**

The mountain is exactly where it was. Same shape, same stone.

**F11**

Everything that fell on it soaked in.

[PAUSE 12 SECONDS]

Note on this part: steady all the way through. No swell on the storm, no
relief when the sun comes out. It is all just weather.

Recording line by line helps you here. Each take is short, so it is harder to
build the drama the script does not want.

---

# PART G — Leaving

**G1**

One more moment with the mountain before you go.

**G2**

The sun is on it now. The air is clear.

**G3**

Birds are chirping in the trees again.

**G4**

Butterflies are chasing each other over the flowers.

[PAUSE 8 SECONDS]

**G5**

Let the mountain fade now.

**G6**

Keep the stillness. That part was yours.

[PAUSE 8 SECONDS]

**G7**

Feel the chair again. Feel your hands.

**G8**

Wiggle your fingers, and your toes.

[PAUSE 5 SECONDS]

**G9**

When you are ready, open your eyes.

**G10**

The weather will keep changing today.

**G11**

You can let it come and go, the way the mountain does.

Note on G8: this is the one light line in the whole script. A small smile is
allowed there, and nowhere else.

Note on G10 and G11: quiet and ordinary. Do not lift them into a finish.

---

# What is never spoken

| On screen | Why it is silent |
| --- | --- |
| The X, and any button label | A voice reading a button is the app talking about itself. |
| The screen title, if there is one | It is a label, not part of the script. |

---

**If a line changes, its take changes with it.** A screen whose words and voice
differ is worse than a slightly clumsy line: the person is trying to follow one
thing and is handed two. Changing a line here means re-recording that one take —
or not changing the line.

Because every line is its own file, that now costs one take instead of a whole
session. On a ten minute script that is the difference between a fix and a
re-shoot.
