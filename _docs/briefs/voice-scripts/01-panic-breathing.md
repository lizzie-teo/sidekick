# 01 — Panic breathing

The person hearing this is in the middle of a panic attack.

## Important — this one is different

This is **not** one long recording. Every line is its own small file.

The app plays them at different times, in different orders, depending on what
the person taps. So record each line separately and clearly.

There are no long silences to leave in this script. The app handles the timing.

## One recording = one screen

This is the rule the whole script is built on.

The app shows **one block of text at a time**, in one place on the screen. Each
block has exactly one recording, and that recording says that block and nothing
else.

So a recording must never run on into the next block. If two sentences appear on
screen together, they are one recording. If they appear one after the other,
they are two recordings.

Part C is the place this matters most. Each answer is **two screens**, so each
answer is **two recordings** — an `a` file and a `b` file. They were once
recorded as one performance and had to be cut apart later. Do not do that again.

## The voice for this script

- Slower than the others. Slower than feels right.
- Very calm. Nothing urgent in it, ever.
- No worry in your voice. Worry spreads.
- Short lines. Let each one land and stop.

## Do not

- Do not sound like you are in a hurry.
- Do not sound concerned.
- Do not stress the word "not" hard. Say it plainly.

## Leave a little air

- Leave about half a second of silence at the **end** of every take.
- Do **not** leave silence at the start. The app plays the file the moment the
  words go up, so leading silence is the screen looking broken.

---

# PART A — The opening beats

Three screens, one after the other. These are the first thing the person hears.

**A1** — screen 1

I'm here.

**A2** — screen 2

Let's breathe together.

**A3** — screen 3

Small breaths.

Note on A3: this is an instruction, said kindly. Not a warning. It is the last
thing said before the first breath, and it is never repeated.

---

# PART B — The breathing cues

Two screens that swap back and forth while the person breathes with the
character. They repeat many times, so an odd stress gets annoying fast.

Record them a few times so we can pick the calmest one.

**B1** — the in-breath screen

In through your nose.

**B2** — the out-breath screen

Out slowly, through your mouth.

Note: "slowly" is the important word in B2, but do not push it. Just let it
be slow.

---

# PART C — "What's happening in your body"

The person taps a button saying what they feel. Then they get **two screens**,
one after the other, and they tap to move from the first to the second.

So every answer below is **two separate recordings**, `a` then `b`.

Record all five pairs. Only one pair is ever played.

The job of these lines is to explain the body. Calm, plain, matter of fact.
Like a nurse who has seen this a hundred times. Kind, but not soft.

## C1 — My heart is racing

**C1a** — screen 1

Your heart is beating fast to move blood to your arms and legs. That's all
it's doing.

**C1b** — screen 2

It feels awful. It isn't hurting you. It slows down on its own.

## C2 — I can't get a full breath

**C2a** — screen 1

Your chest muscles have tightened, so a breath feels unfinished. You are still
taking in all the air you need.

**C2b** — screen 2

Reaching for a bigger breath makes it tighter. A long, slow breath out is what
loosens it.

## C3 — I feel like fainting

**C3a** — screen 1

Fainting happens when blood pressure drops. Right now yours has gone up, not
down.

**C3b** — screen 2

The light-headed feeling is fast breathing, not a warning. It eases as the
out-breath gets longer.

## C4 — My hands are tingling

**C4a** — screen 1

Breathing fast changes the mix of gases in your blood, and that makes hands,
feet and lips tingle.

**C4b** — screen 2

It is harmless, and it fades within a few minutes of slower breathing.

## C5 — The general one

Played when the person did not say what they feel.

**C5a** — screen 1

Whatever your body is doing right now, it is doing it to protect you.

**C5b** — screen 2

It feels awful. It isn't hurting you. It settles on its own.

---

# PART D — Softening

Three screens, one after the other. Played after Part C, for everyone.

**D1**

This feeling is horrible, but it can't hurt you.

**D2**

It has peaked before, and it came down before.

**D3**

You do not have to make it stop. It stops by itself.

---

# PART E — Company

Three screens. Warmer than Part D. This is the friend part. Still quiet.

**E1**

You're doing the best you can.

**E2**

You are still breathing with me.

**E3**

You do not have to feel calm. You only have to stay.

Note on E1 and E3: these are **not** praise. Do not sound proud of them. Say
them the way you would tell a friend a plain fact.

---

# PART F — The end

Two screens.

**F1**

You have been breathing with me for a few minutes now.

**F2**

I'll stay as long as you want. There's no rush to go.

Note: F2 is the last thing they hear. Keep it low and open. Do not make it
sound like goodbye.

---

# What is never spoken

Some things on screen have no recording, on purpose. Do not record them.

| On screen | Why it is silent |
| --- | --- |
| "What's happening in your body?" and its four buttons | A question screen. The person is choosing, not listening. |
| "Next" / "I'm alright now" | Buttons. A voice reading a button is the app talking about itself. |
| "That's enough for now" / "Keep breathing with me" | Same. |

`assets/audio/_source/` holds the un-cut C pairs and the original F2, kept for
reference. That folder is **not** shipped in the app.

**If a line changes, its recording changes with it.** A screen whose words and
voice differ is worse than a slightly clumsy line: the reader is trying to
follow one instruction and is handed two. So changing a line in
`breathing_script.dart`, `sensation.dart` or `BreathingViewModel.leadIn` means
re-cutting its clip — or not changing the line.
