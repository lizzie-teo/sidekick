# Read me first

Four scripts to record. Please read this page before you start.

## What this is

Sidekick is a mental health app. These are the words a person hears when they
are having a hard time: a panic attack, a low day, or feeling wound up.

You are not a narrator. You are a friend sitting next to them.

## The voice

- Low. Warm. Slow.
- Quiet, like talking to someone who has just woken up.
- Steady volume all the way through. No building up, no dropping off.
- Drop your pitch slightly at the end of a line. Do not lift it.

## Things to avoid

| Do not | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Do not smile through it | It sounds like a cheerful advert |
| Do not sound sorry for them | Pity makes people feel worse |
| Do not sound like a yoga teacher | No breathy, floaty, "special" voice |
| Do not lift the last word of a line | A lift sounds like a question |
| Do not speed up near the end | People notice, and it feels like being rushed |
| Do not add words | Not even "just", "now", or "okay" |

## How to read the pauses

Lines in square brackets like this are **not spoken**:

    [PAUSE 8 SECONDS]

**You do not record them.** They tell the app how long to hold that screen
before the next line arrives. They are printed so you can see the shape of the
script, and so you know when a long silence is coming for the listener.

You never have to count or time anything. Two lines with no pause between them
still get a short gap, and the app puts that in too.

## Every line is its own file

All four scripts work the same way. **We never cut a recording into pieces.**

Every spoken line has a label like **A1**, **D4** or **H**. Each label is one
take and one file. You record them one at a time and stop between them. There
is a "The takes" table at the bottom of each script listing every one.

**If a line has a label, it gets its own file.** That label is the whole
instruction. Nothing else marks it.

We used to record one long performance and cut it up. It went wrong: the cuts
landed in the wrong places, and one changed word meant going back to the whole
session. This way costs you a little more time on the day and saves all of it.

### What that changes for you

- The `[PAUSE 8 SECONDS]` lines are **not recorded**. They tell the app how
  long to hold the screen. You do not time anything.
- A fluffed line costs one line. Say it again. You never re-run a part.
- No silence at the start of a take. The app plays the file the moment the
  words go up, so leading silence looks like a broken app.
- About half a second of air at the end of every take.
- Some takes are played more than once in the same script. The script says so.
  Record them **once**. The app repeats the same file, which is how we make
  a repeated line sound identical.

### Staying the same across many takes

This is the one risk of recording line by line. Over thirty or forty takes a
voice drifts.

- Read the whole script out loud once before recording anything.
- Then record in order, front to back, in one sitting.
- Do not move the mic or change your distance part way.
- If you have to stop and come back, tell us where you stopped.

## Files

| File | Script | Takes | On screen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `01-panic-breathing.md` | Panic attack. Short lines. | 23 | about 2 min |
| `02-wound-up.md` | Muscle tighten and stop | 33 | 4 min 25 |
| `03-low-day.md` | Kindness for a low day | 38 | 7 min |
| `04-mountain.md` | Mountain meditation | 64 | 10 min 30 |

158 takes in all, and every one is short. The "on screen" column is how long
the listener sits there, not how long you record for.

Record them in that order. The panic one is shortest and sets the voice for
the rest. The mountain one is longest and hardest to keep even, so it comes
last.

## Recording notes

- Work through each script in order, front to back, in one sitting.
- Please give us 10 seconds of room tone at the top, before you speak.
- If you fluff a line, just do that take again. Nothing else is affected.
- Do not go back and re-do a whole part. Just the take.
- Flat, dry room. No music, no reverb.
- Same mic, same distance, same room for all four scripts. They play in one
  app, so they must sound like one person on one day.

## Questions

Anything unclear, ask before recording it. A line read the wrong way is worse
than a line not recorded.
