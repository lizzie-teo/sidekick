# 02 — Wound up: tighten, and stop

About **4 minutes 25** on screen. Your recording will be much shorter, because
you do not record the silences.

The person hearing this is angry or wound up. They may be pacing about. They
may be standing up.

## What happens

They tighten a part of their body, hold it, then stop suddenly. Then they rest
for a long time. Four rounds: hands, shoulders, jaw, then all of it.

## Important — every line is its own file

This is **not** one long recording, and we do not cut it up afterwards.

Every line below has a label like **A1** or **C4**. Each label is one take and
one file. Record them one at a time, in order, and stop between them.

What that means for you:

- **The `[PAUSE]` lines are not recorded.** They are not silence you leave in.
  They are the app holding the screen still. You do not have to time anything.
- **A fluffed line costs one line.** Say it again and we keep the better take.
  You never re-run a part.
- **Do not leave silence at the start of a take.** The app plays the file the
  moment the words go on screen, so leading silence looks like a broken app.
- **Leave about half a second at the end** of every take.

## Two takes are played more than once

The exercise is a rhythm. The same two lines come round every round, and they
must sound the same every time.

So you record them **once**, and the app plays the same file each round:

| Take | Says | Played |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H** | Hold. | 4 times — hands, shoulders, jaw, all of it |
| **S** | Breathe out, and stop. | 3 times — shoulders, jaw, all of it |

The hands round is the exception. It says "Breathe out, and stop **all at
once**", because that is where the sudden stop is taught. After that the
pattern is set, so the rest use the short one. That take is **B4** and it is
recorded on its own.

These two are the most repeated takes in the script. Get them flat and calm,
and record each a few times so we can pick the best.

## The voice for this script

- Calm and even. Not soothing, not sleepy.
- Plain and practical, like a good PE teacher who is not shouting.
- The rest lines get softer and slower than the instruction lines.

## Do not

- Do not sound like you are telling them off.
- Do not push on "Hold." Say it flat and let the silence do the work.
- Do not sound pleased when they stop. Nothing here is a reward.
- Never say "deep breath". The only breathing line is "Breathe out, and stop."

## Staying the same across 33 takes

This is the one risk of recording line by line. Over 33 takes a voice drifts.

- Read the whole script through once out loud before you record anything.
- Then record in order, front to back, in **one sitting**.
- Do not move the mic, change the room, or change your distance part way.
- If you have to stop and come back, tell us where you stopped.

## How to read the pauses

`[PAUSE 6 SECONDS]` is **not spoken and not recorded**. It tells us how long
the app holds that screen. It is printed so you can see the shape of the
exercise.

Some of them are long. Twelve seconds feels very long in a booth. That is
correct — it is the resting part of the exercise, and it is the part that
works. You do not have to sit through it.

# PART A — Settling

**A1**

Standing or sitting, put both feet on the floor.

**A2**

Your hands can hang, or rest on your legs.

**A3**

Your eyes can close, or stay here.

[PAUSE 5 SECONDS]

**A4**

You are going to tighten a few parts of you, and then stop.

**A5**

Tighten enough to feel it. No more than that.

**A6**

Anything you would rather leave alone, leave heavy.

[PAUSE 6 SECONDS]

Note on A1: this works standing or sitting, and the script never asks which.
Do not read it as if the person is in a chair. A lot of them are pacing.

---

# PART B — Hands

**B1**

Start with your hands.

**B2**

Close them into fists.

**B3**

The rest of you stays heavy.

**H** — first of four plays

Hold.

[PAUSE 6 SECONDS]

**B4**

Breathe out, and stop all at once.

**B5**

Your fists are uncurling.

[PAUSE 12 SECONDS]

**B6**

Your fingers are where they fell.

**B7**

Warm, or heavy, or tingling. Or nothing much.

[PAUSE 12 SECONDS]

Note on B4: this is the only round that says "all at once". It teaches the
sudden stop. Say those three words plainly — do not make them a flourish.

---

# PART C — Shoulders

**C1**

Now your shoulders.

**C2**

Lift them up towards your ears.

**H** — second play. Not recorded again.

Hold.

[PAUSE 6 SECONDS]

**S** — first of three plays

Breathe out, and stop.

**C3**

Your shoulders are dropping.

[PAUSE 12 SECONDS]

**C4**

Your arms are hanging from them.

**C5**

Heavy, or warm, or soft. Or nothing much.

[PAUSE 12 SECONDS]

---

# PART D — Jaw

**D1**

Now your jaw.

**D2**

Press your teeth together, gently.

**H** — third play. Not recorded again.

Hold.

[PAUSE 6 SECONDS]

**S** — second play. Not recorded again.

Breathe out, and stop.

**D3**

Your jaw is coming loose.

[PAUSE 12 SECONDS]

**D4**

Your mouth can rest open a little.

**D5**

Loose, or heavy, or warm. Or nothing much.

[PAUSE 12 SECONDS]

Note on D2: say "gently" clearly. People clench their teeth too hard here and
it hurts them.

---

# PART E — All of it

**E1**

Now all of it at once.

**E2**

Fists. Shoulders. Jaw.

**E3**

Tighten.

**H** — fourth play. Not recorded again.

Hold.

[PAUSE 6 SECONDS]

**S** — third play. Not recorded again.

Breathe out, and stop.

**E4**

All of it is loosening.

[PAUSE 15 SECONDS]

**E5**

The floor, or the chair, is holding all of it.

[PAUSE 12 SECONDS]

Note on E2: "Fists. Shoulders. Jaw." is three short beats with a small gap
between each. Not a list read quickly. It is one take, not three.

---

# PART F — Leaving

**F1**

You can stay here for as long as you want.

[PAUSE 10 SECONDS]

**F2**

When you are ready, let your eyes come back to the room.

**F3**

You can open your hands like that whenever you want to.

Note on F3: this is an offer, not homework. Light and easy. Then stop.

---

# What is never spoken

| On screen | Why it is silent |
| --- | --- |
| The X, and any button label | A voice reading a button is the app talking about itself. |
| The screen title, if there is one | It is a label, not part of the script. |

---

**If a line changes, its take changes with it.** A screen whose words and voice
differ is worse than a slightly clumsy line: the person is trying to follow one
instruction and is handed two. Changing a line here means re-recording that one
take — or not changing the line.

Because every line is its own file, that now costs one take instead of a whole
session. That is the main reason this script is built this way.
