# The line, explained

What opens when somebody taps a check-in alert and then wants more. All
forty-four are written. Voice rules are `_docs/kind-writing-style.md`; the
lines themselves are `_docs/briefs/affirmation-lines.md`.

## What it is

A sheet that slides up over Home. The sidekick stays visible behind it, the
line is at the top, three short parts under it, and one way out.

Nothing is asked, nothing is answered, nothing is stored. Closing it puts the
reader back on Home with the same line still there.

**Reading it is optional and always was.** The line on the lock screen is the
whole message on its own. Somebody who never taps has missed nothing. This is
for the evening where somebody reads a line, thinks "is that actually true?",
and wants the answer.

## The shape comes from the source, not from me

Two drafts invented prose to explain each line and neither made sense. The
assertiveness handout already holds the explanation, sitting in the column
this file had been told to ignore.

`_docs/briefs/ASSERTIVENESS SKILLS TRAINING l.md` is two columns wide:

| Mistaken Traditional Assumption | Your LEGITIMATE Right |
| --- | --- |
| You should always try to accommodate others. If you don't, they won't be there when you need them. | You have the right to say "no." |

The right on its own is an assertion. **The pair is an argument**, and the
left-hand column is the useful half: it names the rule the reader is actually
running on. You cannot put down a belief you cannot see, which is why naming
it is the standard move in assertiveness training in the first place.

So every sheet is three short parts:

| Part | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| **The rule you were given** | The belief, in plainer words. Said as something handed to the reader, never as something they believe. Eight sheets say **What it sounds like** instead -- see the thought shapes below. |
| **Why it does not hold** | One reason, with a real example where there is one. |
| **Closer to the truth** | The line, said plainly. |

### Where the other seventeen came from

Only nineteen of the lines are rights with a handout row. The rest needed
their rule naming from somewhere, and the Centre for Clinical Interventions in
Perth publishes free clinical workbooks built on exactly the same two-column
idea.

Its *Building Self-Compassion* module, on barriers to self-compassion, lists
the beliefs that keep self-criticism running -- that criticism motivates you,
toughens you up, pushes you to excel, keeps you humble; and that kindness to
yourself would make you lazy, self-indulgent and undisciplined. It then gives
the counter-arguments, and two of them are used repeatedly below:

- **It is not the criticism that helps, it is taking action** -- and action is
  available without the criticism.
- **You would never say it to a friend or a child.** If it worked, you would.

`Rest is allowed before it is earned` and `Starting badly is still starting`
are both straight out of that material.

### This reverses an earlier rule, on purpose and only here

`_docs/briefs/affirmation-lines.md` says the mistaken-assumption column must
never be used, because a line that repeats a mistaken belief plants it. **That
still binds every lock-screen line and it is not changing.**

It does not bind the sheet, and the difference is consent. Somebody tapped to
get here. They are reading on purpose, with the app open, and the belief is
named in order to be answered in the next paragraph. A belief named and
answered is recognition. A belief dropped onto a lock screen with nothing
after it is the thing that was banned.

**The naming has to be careful.** It is always "the rule you were given",
never "you think" or "you believe". Nobody is told what is in their head.

## The nineteen rights

One per row of the handout. The left column is the rule, the right column is
what is true instead, and the middle paragraph is the only part that had to be
written.

### 1. You are allowed to come first sometimes.

> **The rule you were given**
> Putting your needs before other people's is selfish.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Selfish means always. Sometimes is not always. And if you never come first, you run out of things to give.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> Your needs count too. Some of the time they can come first.

### 2. Getting things wrong is part of being a person.

> **The rule you were given**
> Making mistakes is shameful. You should have the right answer ready every time.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Nobody has the right answer every time. The ones who look like they do have had more practice.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You are allowed to get it wrong. Getting it wrong is how you find out how to do it.

### 3. You know how you feel better than anyone does.

> **The rule you were given**
> If people do not agree your feelings make sense, then your feelings must be wrong.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> A feeling is not a claim that needs proof. Other people did not live your life. They are missing most of it.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You are the one who knows. Nobody else gets to decide.

### 4. Your opinion is yours to hold.

> **The rule you were given**
> Respect other people's views, especially if they are in charge. Keep your differences to yourself.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Respecting a view is not the same as taking it on. Being in charge does not make somebody right.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can hold your own view. You do not have to hand it over.

### 5. Changing your mind is allowed, even late.

> **The rule you were given**
> You should always be logical and consistent.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Being the same is not a virtue when the facts have changed. Sticking to a bad call to look steady costs more.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can change your mind. Knowing more is a good reason.

### 6. If something felt unfair, you are allowed to say so.

> **The rule you were given**
> Be flexible and adjust. Other people have their reasons, and it is rude to question them.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Sometimes they do have good reasons. Sometimes they do not. There is no way to tell without asking.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can say something felt unfair. Asking is not rude.

### 7. You can stop someone and ask what they meant.

> **The rule you were given**
> Never interrupt. Asking questions shows you did not understand.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Not asking does not hide it. The muddle stays, and it shows up later. Most people would rather be asked.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can stop someone and ask. It gives nothing away.

### 8. You can ask for things to be different.

> **The rule you were given**
> Do not rock the boat. It could get worse.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Not asking has an ending too, and it is that nothing changes. That one rarely gets counted. Asking is not pushing.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You are allowed to ask for a change.

### 9. You are allowed to need somebody.

> **The rule you were given**
> Do not take up people's time with your problems.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> People who care about you would rather know. Being there for each other is most of the point of having people.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can ask for help. Helping each other is what people are for.

### 10. Saying that something hurt is allowed.

> **The rule you were given**
> People do not want to hear that you feel bad, so keep it in.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Kept in, it does not go away. It goes quiet. And the person who hurt you cannot mend what they do not know about.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can say that it hurt.

### 11. You can listen to advice and still not take it.

> **The rule you were given**
> If somebody takes the time to advise you, take it seriously. They are usually right.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> They are guessing from outside your life. Advice often comes from somebody's own worries, or their own story. Yours may be nothing like it.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can listen, say thank you, and not do it.

### 12. Wanting your work noticed is a fair thing to want.

> **The rule you were given**
> Knowing you did well should be enough. Nobody likes a show-off.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Doing well and being noticed are two different things. Wanting the second one does not make you a show-off.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can want your work seen. You can even say so.

### 13. You can say no, and leave it at that.

> **The rule you were given**
> Always fit in with people. If you do not, they will not be there later.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Saying no once does not end a friendship. And when you give a reason, the reason is what people argue with. "You could come for just an hour."
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can say no. You do not have to say why.

### 14. It is alright to want the evening to yourself.

> **The rule you were given**
> Do not be antisocial. Say you would rather be alone and people will think you do not like them.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Wanting time alone is about you, not about them. Most people want it too.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can want an evening on your own.

### 15. "I would rather not" is reason enough.

> **The rule you were given**
> You should always have a good reason for what you feel and do.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Some things you just do not want to do. There is no better reason underneath. Looking for one takes longer than saying it.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You do not have to explain yourself. "I'd rather not" is a real answer.

### 16. Not every problem near you is yours to solve.

> **The rule you were given**
> When somebody is in trouble, you should help them.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> You can care about somebody without taking their problem on. Taking it on can stop them solving it.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can be there without carrying it.

### 17. You are not expected to guess what people need.

> **The rule you were given**
> Be sensitive to what people want, even when they do not tell you.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Nobody can read minds. Guessing goes wrong, and guessing all day is tiring. People can say what they want.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You do not have to work it out in advance.

### 18. Not everybody has to be pleased with you today.

> **The rule you were given**
> It is always a good policy to stay on people's good side.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> You cannot. It would mean agreeing with everyone, and they do not agree with each other. People do not have to agree to like each other.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> Some days people are annoyed and you never find out why. Often it has nothing to do with you.

### 19. Not every message needs an answer tonight.

> **The rule you were given**
> It is not nice to leave people waiting. If you are asked, answer.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Most messages are not urgent. And a tired reply is the worse reply.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can reply tomorrow.

## The other seventeen

No handout row, so the rule had to be named first. Most of them are already
described in the CCI self-compassion module as beliefs that keep self-criticism
running: that criticism motivates, that rest is a reward, that kindness makes
you lazy.

### 20. Hard is allowed to be hard.

> **The rule you were given**
> If you were coping properly, this would not feel hard.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Hard things are hard for everybody. Struggling with one tells you about the thing, not about you.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> It can be hard and you can be fine.

### 21. Tired does not need a reason.

> **The rule you were given**
> You are only allowed to be tired if you can show what tired you out.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Some days you are just tired. There is no reason you can point to. It is still real.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You do not have to prove it.

### 22. Invisible work is still work.

> **The rule you were given**
> Work counts when somebody can see it.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Most of what wears people out leaves no trace. Worrying, planning, keeping the peace, holding it together at work.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> It counted. Nobody had to see it.

### 23. You are allowed to feel exactly this much.

> **The rule you were given**
> Your feelings should match the size of the thing.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Feelings do not arrive measured. And "you're overreacting" is what people say when they want the feeling to stop.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You feel what you feel. There is no correct amount.

### 24. Today is allowed to be a slow one.

> **The rule you were given**
> A day should produce something.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> That is a rule about work, used on a life. Nobody makes something every day. The ones who look like they do are not showing you all of it.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> Some days are slow. That is a day, not a failure.

### 25. You can take up as much room as you need.

> **The rule you were given**
> Do not be a burden. Take up less space.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> People who like you are not keeping count. That is not how liking somebody works.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You are allowed to take up room.

### 26. Rest is allowed before it is earned.

> **The rule you were given**
> Rest is what you get after you finish.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Bodies do not work like that. Resting when you are tired is how more gets done, not less.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can rest now. You do not have to have earned it.

### 27. Tonight does not have to sort out tomorrow.

> **The rule you were given**
> If you do not work it out now, you will be caught out.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Going over a problem at night rarely solves it. It just runs it again. The morning is a better time to think.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> It can wait until tomorrow.

### 28. It is a human way to feel.

> **The rule you were given**
> Other people do not feel like this. Something is wrong with you.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Other people's insides are not on show. Only yours are, to you. That is not a fair match, and everybody makes it.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> Plenty of people feel exactly this. Right now.

### 29. A thought showed up. You did not choose it.

> **The rule you were given**
> A thought is yours, so it says something about you.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Minds throw up all sorts. Some of it you disagree with. Having a thought is not the same as picking it.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> A thought arriving tells you nothing about who you are.

### 30. Minds wander. That is what minds do.

> **The rule you were given**
> A mind that wanders is a mind you have lost control of.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Minds do this all day. Everybody's does. So does the mind of somebody who has meditated for years.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> A wandering mind is a mind working the way minds work.

### 31. You do not have to answer every thought.

> **The rule you were given**
> A thought that turns up is addressed to you and wants an answer.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> You cannot stop thoughts showing up. That part is not up to you. But you do not have to deal with each one.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can notice a thought and carry on with what you were doing.

### 32. You got through today. Nothing more was needed.

> **The rule you were given**
> Getting through a day does not count as doing anything.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Some days, getting through it is the work. It takes something. Nobody sees it. It still happened.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You got to the end of it. That was enough.

### 33. Starting badly is still starting.

> **The rule you were given**
> If you cannot do it properly, do not start.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Nothing starts well. The good version is a bad version that got worked on.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> A bad start is still a start. Starting when you know it will be rough is the harder version.

### 34. Half done still counts.

> **The rule you were given**
> Unfinished means it does not count.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Half the washing up is half less washing up. It does not come back because you stopped.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> What you did is still done.

### 35. Slow is still forward.

> **The rule you were given**
> If it is not fast, it is not progress.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Speed is a comparison. Progress is not. Something moving slowly is still moving.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> Slow still counts as forward.

### 36. The chair is holding you. Let it do the work.

> **The rule you were given**
> You have to hold yourself up.
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> The chair is already doing it. Most people hold their shoulders and back tight all day and never notice. They are holding up something already held.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can stop holding. It has you.

## The eight thought shapes

Drawn from `_docs/briefs/17 UNHELPFUL THINKING STYLES.md`, which is Leahy's
list of cognitive distortions. A different shape of source, so a different
shape of sheet.

The assertiveness handout names a **rule about how to behave**. This one names
a **shape a thought takes**. There is no rule to put in the first part, so
these sheets open with the thought itself, under "What it sounds like".

### These must never diagnose

The biggest risk in the whole file lives here, and it is worth stating
plainly. **Naming a distortion is telling the reader their thinking is
faulty.** "You are catastrophising" is a diagnosis, delivered by an app, to
somebody having a hard evening, about the inside of their own head. It breaks
the oldest rule in these files: never tell the reader something about
themselves that the app cannot know.

Three rules follow, and all eight sheets hold them:

- **The shape is never named.** Not "catastrophising", not "mind reading", not
  "overgeneralising". The words do not appear anywhere the reader can see.
- **The sheet describes the thought, never the thinker.** "A guess about
  tomorrow is still a guess" is about the guess. "You always assume the
  worst" would be about the reader, and it would be wrong as often as right.
- **The thought is quoted, not attributed.** The first part is in quotation
  marks, as a sentence somebody might hear, never as "what you are telling
  yourself".

### One was written and thrown away

**Emotional reasoning** -- treating a feeling as proof of a fact -- is on the
source list and is not here.

It contradicts line 6, "You know how you feel better than anyone does",
which tells the reader they are the authority on their own feelings. The real
distinction is between the feeling being genuine and the feeling proving
something about the world, and that difference is too fine to carry on a lock
screen. Got wrong, it reads as the app telling somebody their feelings are
not to be trusted, which is the opposite of what line 6 exists to say.

### 37. Once is not always.

> **What it sounds like**
> "That always happens to me." "I always get this wrong."
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> One time is one time. "Always" turns up to protect you. It is trying to get you ready for next time, so it makes one go sound like a rule.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> It happened once. That is all you know so far.

### 38. Most things are not all or nothing.

> **What it sounds like**
> "It was a complete waste of time." "Nobody likes me."
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Almost nothing is all of anything. Most of it sits in the middle, and the middle is harder to see when you are tired.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> Most things are a mix. Some of it went badly. Some of it did not.

### 39. A guess about tomorrow is still a guess.

> **What it sounds like**
> "It is going to go badly. I already know how this ends."
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Nobody can see tomorrow. A guess made on a bad evening sounds far more certain than it is.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You do not know yet, and nor does anybody else. Tomorrow can be dealt with tomorrow.

### 40. A what-if has no answer that will do.

> **What it sounds like**
> "But what if it goes wrong? What if I cannot cope?"
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Answer one and another one turns up. No answer settles it. What the question wants is to be certain, and certain is not something anybody gets.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You can leave the question sitting there.

### 41. You cannot know what they were thinking.

> **What it sounds like**
> "They think I am an idiot. They were annoyed with me."
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> Nobody can see inside somebody else's head. A guess made on a bad day tends to be the unkind one.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You do not know. Nobody can know what somebody else is thinking.

### 42. You cannot go back and do it differently.

> **What it sounds like**
> "I should have said something else. I should have known."
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> That judges an old decision using what you know now. You did not know it then. It is not a fair test.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You did it with what you had at the time.

### 43. One bad go is not a description of you.

> **What it sounds like**
> "I am useless. I am a bad friend."
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> A whole person does not fit in one word. And that word only shows up after something goes wrong. It never shows up after something goes well.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> Something went badly. That is the size of it. (That is the size of it doesn't feel like it says anything)

### 44. It still counts, even if it was easy.

> **What it sounds like**
> "That does not count. Anybody could have done that."
>
> **Why it does not hold**
> "Anybody could have done it" is a way of making it smaller. It still needed doing, and you are the one who did it.
>
> **Closer to the truth**
> You did it. That is the whole test.

## Two notes from the review

**"Learning from mistakes helps us grow" was asked for and written carefully.**
Sheet 2 now ends "Getting it wrong is how you find out how to do it." The
obvious version -- *mistakes are how we grow* -- puts a condition back on:
if a mistake is alright because you learned from it, then a mistake you
learned nothing from is still shameful, which is the rule the sheet is
answering. The wording above makes getting it wrong part of the method, not
the price of being forgiven.

**Nine sheets stopped assuming things about the reader.** One was caught in
review and the other eight came out of an audit of all thirty-six afterwards.
It is the file's oldest rule, arriving in a new disguise: never tell the
reader something about themselves that the app cannot know.

| Was | Now |
| --- | --- |
| Somebody is annoyed with you. | Some days people are annoyed and you never find out why. |
| You just stay lost. | The confusion stays. |
| …which is the thing you are unhappy about. | …which is the other outcome nobody counts. |
| And you would want them to tell you. | Most people would want to be told, if it were the other way round. |
| When you try you get it wrong, and it wears you out. | Guessing goes wrong, and guessing all day is tiring. |
| An answer given when you are empty. | A tired reply. |
| The counting is happening in your head. | That is not how liking somebody works. |
| Tomorrow you will know more and be awake. | The morning is a better time to think. |
| You see other people's outsides and your own insides. | Other people's insides are not on show. Only yours are, to you. |

The pattern in every one of them is the same. The sentence was true of most
people most of the time, which is exactly what makes it easy to write and easy
to miss. It still lands as the app telling somebody what is going on in their
head, their week or their body, and the app does not know.

**The sheet that started it.** A draft
said "somebody is annoyed with you", which quietly hands the reader
responsibility for a mood they had no part in. People are annoyed for their
own reasons and usually never say which, so the sheet now says that instead.
It is the same rule as the one behind the whole file: never tell the reader
something about themselves that the app cannot know.

**Two lines changed, not just their sheets.** "A thought turned up. You did
not order it" became "A thought showed up. You did not choose it" -- ordering
is something you do in a shop, and choosing is the actual point. "Half of it
done is a real amount" became "Half done still counts", which is what
somebody would say out loud.

## Seven lines were cut writing this

Each one failed the same test: no rule behind it, or a rule already answered
by a line that does it better. A line with no rule behind it cannot have a
sheet, and a set where some lines open something and others do not is a set
where tapping is a gamble.

| Cut | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Some days ask for more than they say they will. | The nearest rule is "you should be able to predict what a day will cost", which nobody actually believes. |
| Carrying something quietly is still carrying it. | Same rule as "Invisible work is still work", which says it better. |
| If today was a lot, that makes sense. | A conditional kindness, not a belief. Nothing to argue with. |
| It is fine to want an easy evening. | Same rule as "Today is allowed to be a slow one" and "It is alright to want the evening to yourself". |
| Nobody gets through a week without a bad hour. | Same rule as "It is a human way to feel". |
| You are the room, not the weather in it. | A metaphor, which rule 4 of the style guide bans. It was the only one left in the set. |
| Starting with the small things is a real plan. | Same rule as "Starting badly is still starting", which says it better. |

## Every sheet says what family it is from

Added 19 September 2026. A small label above the line, so the reader can see
what kind of thing they have opened before they read it.

| Category | Lines |
| --- | --- |
| When it was hard | 3 |
| Things you're allowed | 19 |
| Your own pace | 5 |
| Other people feel this too | 1 |
| When a thought won't go | 11 |
| Small still counts | 4 |
| Your body | 1 |

**No category names the technique behind it.** Not "assertiveness training",
not "cognitive distortions", not "self-compassion". A category is there to
orient somebody, not to tell them which textbook they are in -- and a reader
who learns they are doing CBT at 8:30 pm has been handed a fact about the app
rather than about themselves. A test pins that the words never appear.

The category also answers a worry recorded twice in
`_docs/briefs/affirmation-lines.md`: that a set where every line teaches reads
as a course. A visible label makes that more obvious, not less, and it is a
fair trade -- somebody who can see the seven families can tell that the app is
not repeating itself, which is the thing a course actually feels like.

## The third heading is "Closer to the truth"

It was "What is true instead". That claims the last word, and most of these
sheets are about nuance rather than swapping one certainty for another -- the
all-or-nothing sheet in particular says most things are a mix, under a heading
announcing what is true. The heading undid the sheet that earned it.

## Built

The sheet is `lib/features/dashboard/widgets/affirmation_sheet.dart` and the
words are in `lib/features/dashboard/models/affirmation_explanations.dart`,
generated from this file.

Two of the open questions below were answered while building:

- **The three headings are on screen.** They carry the argument, and without
  them the three parts run together into one paragraph that reads as a
  lecture.
- **The line on Home is tappable.** Nothing marks it as a button -- somebody
  who only wants to read the line should not be handed a thing to press -- but
  it means the writing is reachable without waiting for an evening alert.
  Easily reversed if Home starts feeling like something to read rather than
  something to look at.

A test pins that every line in the set has an explanation and that no
explanation exists for a line that is not in the set. That is what stops the
two files drifting apart, which nothing else would catch.

## Open questions

- **Does the sheet want the character in it, or only behind it?** She is on
  Home underneath. Putting her inside as well would be two of her.
- **The rights are still nineteen of thirty-six.** Over half. It was already
  the largest group and the cuts fell elsewhere again.
